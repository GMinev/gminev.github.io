I work where media research and media practice meet. I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Media and Public Communications at UNWE in Sofia and, since 2014, Editor-in-Chief of Money.bg, one of Bulgaria's leading business news sites. This combination is deliberate: it lets me study the changes in news and economic information while dealing with them every day as a media manager.

As a researcher, I look at how search engines, social platforms and generative AI are changing the way news and economic information reach the public, and what this means for the business of journalism, for its role as a trusted intermediary and for the skills audiences need. As an editor, I face the same questions in practice: where readers come from, how a newsroom earns its money and what keeps an audience's trust. I bring what I observe in practice into my research, and I take what research shows back into the newsroom.

Forthcoming book

UNWE Press, forthcoming

Economic Journalism: Past and Perspectives

The monograph builds on my doctoral research on business journalism in a convergent media environment. It draws a clear line between economic and business journalism, traces the history of economic journalism in Bulgaria through its successive stages and follows how digital forms, technologies and platforms have changed the way business news is produced, distributed and paid for.

The book is written in Bulgarian, with students and young journalists in mind. No new textbook on the subject has appeared in Bulgarian for about ten years, and the book will serve as a core resource for my course Media and Business.

Current projects

Early stage

After the Click: Traffic Decline and the Future of Bulgarian Online News

For years, online news relied on a simple exchange: search engines and social platforms sent readers to news sites, and publishers turned that traffic into advertising revenue. That exchange is changing. Search engines increasingly answer questions directly, and AI tools offer ready-made summaries, so users often never open the original source.

This project looks at how the shift affects the Bulgarian news market, which is small and depends on advertising and on traffic from platforms. It traces the audiences of leading news websites over recent years and the routes by which readers reach them, and asks which revenue models could replace mass traffic. It also links media economics with media literacy. When journalism loses direct contact with its audience, it also loses the chance to explain where information comes from and why it can be trusted.

Early stage

The Zero-Click Generation? How Young People Get Information About Money

Young people increasingly get answers where they first meet them: in a search result, from an AI chatbot or in a video by a content creator. This project asks whether the same is true for information about money, where the stakes are real and it can be hard to tell promotion from advice.

The study will compare how university students in Bulgaria judge the same financial claim when it comes from a news outlet, an AI tool or a content creator: how far they trust it, whether they would check it and whether they would open the source. A survey will be followed by focus groups. The project will also ask whether media literacy or financial literacy better explains these choices.

Teaching

My aim in every course is to show students the direct link between theory and what happens in real newsrooms and media businesses. I draw on my own work as an editor, use real audience data as teaching material and bring students into practical projects.
